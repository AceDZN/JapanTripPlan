## Important: Jewish Calendar Conflict

### October 1-4, 2026 overlaps with Sukkot / Shemini Atzeret / Simchat Torah

The first days of your trip fall during the tail end of the Sukkot holiday season:

| Date | Day of Week | Jewish Holiday | Restrictions |
|------|-------------|---------------|-------------|
| Sep 25 (Fri) eve | Friday | Sukkot begins (1st day Yom Tov) | Full holiday restrictions |
| Sep 26-27 | Sat-Sun | Sukkot 1st-2nd days | Full holiday restrictions (Diaspora) |
| Sep 28 - Oct 1 | Mon-Thu | Chol HaMoed Sukkot | Intermediate days -- travel permitted |
| **Oct 1 (Thu)** | Thursday | **Hoshana Rabba** | Last day of Chol HaMoed, special prayers |
| **Oct 2 (Fri)** eve | Friday | **Shemini Atzeret begins** | Full Yom Tov restrictions |
| **Oct 3 (Sat)** | Saturday | **Shemini Atzeret / Simchat Torah** | Full Yom Tov + Shabbat (in Israel: combined) |
| **Oct 4 (Sun)** | Sunday | **Simchat Torah** (Diaspora 2nd day) | Full Yom Tov restrictions (Diaspora) |
| Oct 5 (Mon) | Monday | Regular weekday | No restrictions |

**Note for Israeli families:** In Israel, Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah are combined on one day (Oct 2 eve - Oct 3 night). In the Diaspora (including Japan), they are two separate days. Consult with Rabbi Sudakevich about which practice to follow while in Japan.

### Impact on Your Trip
- **Oct 1:** You arrive in Tokyo. This is Hoshana Rabba -- the last day of Sukkot's intermediate days. If arriving during the day, you may want to attend services.
- **Oct 2-3 (Fri-Sat):** Shemini Atzeret + Shabbat. Plan to attend services at Chabad. No travel or activities.
- **Oct 4 (Sun):** Simchat Torah (Diaspora). Another day of celebration at Chabad. Dancing with the Torah -- a wonderful experience!
- **Oct 5 (Mon):** First regular day. Sightseeing begins.

### What the Bar Mitzvah Ceremony Includes (at Chabad)

A typical bar mitzvah at Chabad Tokyo includes:
- **Aliyah la-Torah** -- the boy is called up to the Torah for the first time
- **Reading from the Torah** (if prepared) or reciting the blessings
- **Putting on tefillin** for the first time (usually done on a weekday before the Shabbat celebration)
- **Kiddush celebration** after services -- Chabad typically hosts a festive kiddush
- **Personalized attention** from Rabbi Sudakevich

**Preparation:** Contact Chabad well in advance (3-6 months) to arrange:
- Torah reading preparation (the boy should ideally prepare his portion before the trip)
- Tefillin practice sessions
- Any special requests for the celebration
- Catering for a kiddush/lunch after services

**Note:** Chabad Tokyo hosts about 4-5 bar mitzvahs per year, so they are experienced with traveling families.

### Self-Catering / Budget Kosher Tips

For a family of 4 over 17 days, eating kosher on a budget in Tokyo requires planning:

| Strategy | Details |
|----------|---------|
| **Fish restaurants** | Sushi/sashimi of plain fish (salmon, tuna) with rice -- no shellfish. Many observant travelers eat at conveyor belt sushi restaurants. Consult your rabbi. |
| **Vegetarian/vegan restaurants** | Tokyo has many options. Look for plant-based ramen, vegetable tempura, edamame |
| **Conbini (convenience stores)** | Onigiri with plain tuna/salmon filling, edamame, fruit, packaged salads -- read ingredients |
| **Self-cooking** | If your accommodation has a kitchen, buy kosher basics at National Azabu |
| **Fruit & vegetables** | Always kosher. Japanese fruit is excellent (though expensive) |
| **Pack from Israel** | Bring shelf-stable kosher snacks, instant soups, canned goods for emergencies |

**Important:** Japanese food heavily uses soy sauce (may contain non-kosher ingredients), mirin, dashi (fish stock), and sake in cooking. Even "vegetarian" dishes may contain dashi. When in doubt, ask Chabad for guidance.
