## 1. Konbini (Convenience Store) Food Guide

Japan's convenience stores serve restaurant-quality food at a fraction of the price. There are over 56,000 konbini across Japan -- you will never be more than a few minutes from one. They are open 24/7, have clean restrooms, and accept IC cards (Suica/Pasmo).

### The Big Three: Comparison

| Feature | 7-Eleven | FamilyMart | Lawson |
|---------|----------|------------|--------|
| **Best for** | Onigiri, sandwiches, desserts | Fried chicken (FamiChiki), pasta | Bread/pastries, Karaage-kun |
| **Signature item** | Tamago (egg salad) sandwich | FamiChiki (fried chicken, ~200 yen) | Karaage-kun (fried chicken nuggets, ~250 yen) |
| **Onigiri range** | 130-200 yen | 120-200 yen | 120-200 yen |
| **Bento range** | 400-700 yen | 400-700 yen | 400-700 yen |
| **Desserts** | Roll cakes, purin (pudding) | Famima sweets collection | Uchi Cafe premium line |
| **Hot food counter** | Nikuman (meat buns), oden | FamiChiki, hashbrowns | L-Chiki, oden |
| **Coffee** | Seven Cafe (110 yen) | Famima Cafe (110 yen) | Machi Cafe (110 yen) |
| **ATM** | Yes (international cards) | Limited | Limited |
| **Locations** | ~21,000 | ~16,000 | ~14,000 |

### Must-Try Konbini Items

**Breakfast picks (budget: 400-500 yen per person)**
- Onigiri rice ball (130-170 yen) -- tuna mayo, salmon, and umeboshi (pickled plum) are top flavors
- Tamago sando / egg sandwich (250-300 yen) -- surprisingly creamy and delicious
- Nikuman / steamed meat bun (180-220 yen) -- available from September through spring
- Banana or seasonal fruit (100-150 yen)
- Seven Cafe hot coffee for parents (110 yen regular, 180 yen large)

**Lunch/dinner picks (budget: 400-700 yen per person)**
- Bento box (400-700 yen) -- varieties include karaage, shogayaki (ginger pork), hamburg steak
- Pasta meals (350-550 yen) -- carbonara, meat sauce, mentaiko (cod roe)
- Cup noodles (200-350 yen) -- premium konbini versions are far better than overseas versions
- Oden (hot pot items, 80-150 yen each) -- available October through March, pick individual items from the pot

**Snacks and desserts**
- Onigiri rice balls (130-170 yen)
- FamiChiki or Karaage-kun fried chicken (200-250 yen)
- Purin / custard pudding (200-350 yen)
- Roll cake slices (200-350 yen)
- Ice cream (100-350 yen) -- try Haagen-Dazs Japan-exclusive flavors
- Seasonal sweets -- October features chestnut (kuri) and sweet potato flavors

**Drinks**
- Water / tea (100-160 yen)
- Bottled green tea (100-170 yen) -- Oi Ocha, Iyemon, or Sou Ken Bi Cha
- Canned coffee (130 yen)
- Fresh machine coffee (110-180 yen)
- Juice boxes for kids (100-130 yen)

## 2. Budget Restaurant Chains

These chains are everywhere in Tokyo and Osaka. Most have picture menus or ticket vending machines (shokkenki) at the entrance -- no Japanese needed. Many are open late or 24 hours.

### Gyudon (Beef Bowl) Chains -- The Cheapest Sit-Down Meals

| Chain | Regular Bowl Price | Set Meal Price | Free Extras | Locations | Best For |
|-------|-------------------|----------------|-------------|-----------|----------|
| **Matsuya** | ~430 yen | ~600 yen | Free miso soup with every gyudon | ~1,200 | Cheapest option, free miso soup |
| **Sukiya** | ~430 yen | ~630 yen | Wide topping selection | ~1,900 | Most locations, variety |
| **Yoshinoya** | ~470 yen | ~650 yen | Classic taste since 1899 | ~1,200 | Best classic gyudon flavor |
| **Nakau** | ~430 yen | ~600 yen | Udon/soba side options | ~450 | Combo variety |

**How it works**: Enter the restaurant, use the ticket machine (vending machine with food photos and buttons) near the entrance. Insert money, press the button for your meal, hand the ticket to staff. Food arrives in 2-3 minutes. Water is self-service (free).

**Family of 4 estimate**: 1,800-2,800 yen for a full meal with set menus.

**Recommended**: Matsuya is the best value -- cheapest base prices AND free miso soup with every gyudon order.

## 3. Ramen Guide

Ramen is a must-eat in Japan. Individual bowls range from 650-1,200 yen. For a family of 4, expect 3,000-5,000 yen per ramen meal.

### Budget Ramen Chains

| Chain | Price Range | Style | Notes |
|-------|-------------|-------|-------|
| **Hidakaya** | 400-650 yen | Various | Cheapest ramen chain in Japan |
| **Kourakuen** | 500-750 yen | Various | Large menu, set meals available |
| **Tenkaippin** | 700-900 yen | Chicken broth (kotteri) | Rich, thick broth, unique |
| **Ippudo** | 850-1,100 yen | Hakata tonkotsu | Reliable quality, English menu |
| **Ichiran** | 980-1,200 yen | Hakata tonkotsu | Solo booth experience, worth trying once |

### Must-Try Ramen Experiences

**Ichiran Ramen** (try once for the experience)
- Price: ~980 yen per bowl (base), 1,200+ yen with extras
- The famous solo booth dining experience: each person sits in a private booth separated by dividers, fills out a preference form (noodle firmness, broth richness, garlic level, spice), and slides it through a bamboo curtain to the kitchen
- Locations: Shibuya, Shinjuku, Ikebukuro, Ueno, and more
- Kids love the novelty of the ordering system
- Family of 4: ~4,500-5,500 yen
- Website: [ichiran.com/english](https://ichiran.com/english/)

**Fuunji** (Shinjuku)
- Price: ~1,000 yen
- Famous for tsukemen (dipping noodles) -- thick noodles that you dip into a concentrated broth
- Located near Shinjuku Station south exit
- Expect a line (15-30 min), but it moves fast

**Rokurinsha** (Tokyo Station)
- Price: ~1,000 yen
- Located on Tokyo Ramen Street (underground at Tokyo Station)
- Specializes in tsukemen with a rich seafood-pork broth
- Often has the longest line on Ramen Street

**Hakata Tenjin** (Shinjuku)
- Price: ~650 yen -- one of the cheapest quality ramen bowls in Tokyo
- No-frills Hakata-style tonkotsu ramen
- Great late-night option

### Ramen Tips for Families
- Most ramen shops have small/kids portions for ~500-700 yen
- Water and pickled ginger are free and self-serve
- Slurping noodles is polite and expected in Japan
- Ticket machines at the entrance -- choose and pay before sitting down
- Many shops close when broth runs out (especially popular ones), so go for lunch rather than late dinner

## 4. Conveyor Belt Sushi (Kaitenzushi) -- A Must-Do Family Experience

Conveyor belt sushi is one of the best family dining experiences in Japan. Plates of sushi rotate past on a belt (or are ordered via touchscreen tablet), and you grab what looks good. Each plate is color-coded by price.

### Top Chains Comparison

| Chain | Base Plate Price | Locations | Family-Friendly Features |
|-------|-----------------|-----------|--------------------------|
| **Sushiro** | 120 yen | 650+ (largest chain) | Touch panel in English/Chinese/Korean, seasonal specials |
| **Kura Sushi** | 115 yen | 500+ | Every 5 plates = gachapon game (kids LOVE this), no artificial additives |
| **Hamazushi** | 110 yen | 500+ | 6 types of soy sauce, 100+ sushi varieties, cheapest base price |
| **Genki Sushi** | 110-150 yen | 150+ | Express lane delivery (sushi arrives by shinkansen-shaped tray!) |
| **Ganso Zushi** | 138 yen | limited | Budget standing sushi in Shinjuku/Asakusa |

### What to Expect

- **Ordering**: Use the touchscreen tablet at your table (available in English). Order specific items, or grab plates as they pass on the belt
- **Pricing**: Most plates are 110-220 yen (2 pieces per plate). Premium items (toro, uni, wagyu) may be 330-550 yen
- **Non-sushi options**: Ramen, tempura, french fries, edamame, karaage, cake, ice cream, parfaits -- kids who do not like raw fish will still eat well
- **Payment**: Staff count your plates at the end to calculate the bill

### Family of 4 Estimate

| Appetite | Plates per person | Family total |
|----------|------------------|-------------|
| Light | 6-8 plates | 2,900-3,800 yen |
| Average | 10-12 plates | 4,800-5,800 yen |
| Hungry | 14-18 plates | 6,700-8,600 yen |

### Recommended: Kura Sushi for Families

Kura Sushi is the best choice for families with kids because of the gachapon (capsule toy) game -- after every 5 plates, you get to play a capsule machine game built into the table. Win or lose, kids love it and it turns dinner into an event. All dishes are free from artificial flavors, colors, sweeteners, and preservatives.

- Locations: widespread across Tokyo (Ikebukuro, Shibuya, Shinjuku, Asakusa, Ueno, Akihabara)
- Reservations: use the Kura Sushi app to reserve and skip the line
- App: search "Kura Sushi" in the App Store
- Website: [kurasushi.co.jp](https://www.kurasushi.co.jp/en/)

## 6. Supermarket Strategy -- The Secret Budget Weapon

Japanese supermarkets sell restaurant-quality prepared foods (bento, sushi, tempura, salads, side dishes) at lower prices than restaurants. The real secret is the evening markdown system.

### Evening Discount Timing

| Time | Typical Discount | What to Expect |
|------|-----------------|----------------|
| Before 6:00 PM | Full price | Full selection available |
| 6:00 PM | 10-20% off | Stickers start appearing on side dishes |
| 7:00-8:00 PM | 20-30% off | Most bento and sushi marked down |
| 8:00-9:00 PM | 30-50% off | Significant discounts, selection shrinking |
| 9:00 PM - close | 50% off (hangaku) | Half-price stickers on everything remaining |

### What Gets Discounted
- Bento boxes (normally 400-800 yen, down to 200-400 yen at half price)
- Sushi platters (normally 600-1,500 yen, down to 300-750 yen)
- Sashimi packs
- Tempura and fried foods
- Side dishes (salads, pickles, tofu)
- Meat and fish (for cooking if you have a kitchen)
- Bread and pastries

### Supermarket Chains to Look For

| Chain | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **Life** | Common in central Tokyo, good prepared food section |
| **Seiyu** | Walmart-owned, consistently low prices, open late |
| **Summit** | Good in residential areas |
| **OK Store** | Discount supermarket, already low base prices |
| **Hanamasa** | Wholesale-style, great for bulk snacks |
| **Aeon** | Large stores, often in malls |
| **Don Quijote (food section)** | Open late/24h, snacks and bento available |

### Sample Supermarket Dinner (family of 4, after 8 PM)

| Item | Regular Price | Discount Price (30-50% off) |
|------|--------------|---------------------------|
| 2x Bento boxes | 1,200 yen | 600-840 yen |

| 1x Side salad | 300 yen | 150-210 yen |

### Tips
- Look for the orange/red sticker with a percentage (10%/20%/30%) or the word "hangaku" (half price)
- Supermarkets near train stations are most convenient
- The quality of supermarket sushi in Japan is better than most sushi restaurants outside Japan
- Some supermarkets have eat-in areas with tables
- Bring your own bag or buy one (3-5 yen)

## 10. Money-Saving Food Tips -- Master List

### Before You Eat
1. **Eat konbini breakfast every day** -- saves 500-1,000 yen per person versus restaurant breakfast
2. **Check for depachika discounts after 7 PM** -- department store basements sell premium food at 30-50% off
3. **Supermarket bento after 8 PM** -- half-price stickers on sushi, bento, and prepared foods
4. **Water is free everywhere** -- tap water is safe and delicious. Restaurants always serve free water or tea
5. **Carry a reusable bottle** -- fill at hotel, public fountains, or restaurant water dispensers

### While You Order
6. **Use ticket machines** -- at ramen, gyudon, and udon chains. No tipping, no table service charge
7. **Order set meals (teishoku)** -- rice + soup + main dish is better value than individual items
8. **Kids eat cheaper** -- most chains have kids menus (400-550 yen). Ask for "kodomo menu"
9. **Share large portions** -- some places offer "oomori" (large) for just 50-100 yen extra. Split between 2 kids
10. **Free toppings** -- at gyudon chains: pickled ginger (beni shoga), on-table. At udon chains: green onion, sesame, ginger

### Where You Eat
11. **Avoid tourist-trap restaurants** -- steer clear of places with English touts outside. The best food is where locals eat
12. **Basement floors of malls** -- food courts and prepared food counters are always cheaper than upper-floor restaurants
13. **Standing restaurants (tachinomi/tachigui)** -- standing ramen and sushi bars are 20-40% cheaper than seated equivalents
14. **Train station food** -- ekiben (station bento) are pre-made and reasonably priced (800-1,200 yen)
15. **Nakano Broadway area > Akihabara** for food too -- less tourist markup

### Smart Spending
16. **Tax-free shopping at supermarkets** -- spend 5,000+ yen at participating stores, show passport, save 10% consumption tax on consumables (food/drinks/snacks count!)
17. **Use your IC card (Suica/Pasmo)** -- works at konbini, vending machines, many restaurants. Easier than carrying coins
18. **Daiso/100-yen shops for snacks** -- stock up on Japanese snacks for 110 yen each. Great for the hotel room
19. **Don Quijote for bulk snacks** -- buy family packs of Japanese snacks cheaper than konbini singles
20. **No tipping in Japan** -- ever. The price you see is the price you pay (plus 10% consumption tax, usually already included in displayed prices at restaurants)

## 13. Practical Food Tips for Families

### Dietary Considerations
- **Pork is extremely common** in Japanese cuisine (ramen broth, gyudon toppings, tonkatsu, nikuman filling). If avoiding pork, tell staff: "Butaniku nashi" (no pork) or "Buta nashi de onegaishimasu"
- **Halal/Kosher**: Strictly kosher food is very difficult to find in Tokyo. If this is a concern, konbini onigiri (salmon, tuna, kelp, umeboshi varieties) and seafood options are safer bets. Avoid anything labeled "tonkotsu" (pork bone broth)
- **Allergies**: Most chain restaurants have allergen charts. Ask for "arerugi hyou" (allergy chart)
- **Vegetarian options** are limited but improving. Udon/soba chains offer basic noodle dishes. Note: most dashi (soup base) contains bonito (fish)

### Eating with Kids in Japan
- Most restaurants welcome children warmly
- High chairs available at family restaurants and most chains
- Japanese portions are smaller than Western portions -- kids often eat a full adult meal
- No children's discount at most ramen/gyudon chains, but portions are naturally affordable
- Kid-friendly failsafe foods: onigiri, karaage (fried chicken), curry rice, udon, edamame, tamagoyaki (sweet egg)
- Ice cream and desserts are everywhere and cheap (100-400 yen)

### Payment Tips
- **Cash is still king** at smaller restaurants, street vendors, and some ramen shops
- **IC card (Suica/Pasmo)** works at konbini, vending machines, and many chain restaurants
- **Credit cards** accepted at family restaurants, department stores, and larger chains
- **No tipping** -- it is considered rude in Japan
