## 1. Weather & What to Pack

### October Weather Overview

October is one of the best months to visit Tokyo. Summer heat and humidity have faded, typhoon frequency drops sharply, and the air is crisp and pleasant for walking.

| Metric | Early Oct (1-10) | Mid Oct (11-17) |
|--------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **High temp** | 23-25C (73-77F) | 20-23C (68-73F) |
| **Low temp** | 16-18C (61-64F) | 14-16C (57-61F) |
| **Rainfall** | Moderate (typhoon tail-end) | Decreasing |
| **Humidity** | 60-70% | 55-65% |
| **Daylight** | ~11.5 hours | ~11.2 hours |
| **Sunrise/Sunset** | ~5:35 / 17:15 | ~5:45 / 17:00 |

### Typhoon Awareness

- October is the **tail end** of typhoon season (peak: July-September)
- On average, fewer than 1 typhoon affects Tokyo in October
- Early October carries slightly higher risk than mid-to-late October
- **What to do if a typhoon hits**:
  - Trains will stop running (check NAVITIME or JR East app for status)
  - Stay indoors at your accommodation
  - Stock up on konbini food/water in advance if warnings are issued
  - Typhoons typically pass within 24-48 hours
  - Plan indoor alternatives: shopping malls, arcades, themed cafes
- Monitor the Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) at jma.go.jp for typhoon tracking

### What to Pack

| Category | Items | Notes |
|----------|-------|-------|
| **Layers** | Light long-sleeve shirts, thin sweater/cardigan, 1 light jacket | Mornings and evenings cool down; daytime can still be warm |
| **Rain gear** | Compact umbrella + light rain jacket per person | Konbini sell clear umbrellas for ~100-500 yen if you forget |
| **Footwear** | Comfortable walking shoes (broken in!) | You will walk 15,000-25,000 steps daily; slip-on shoes are ideal for temple visits (shoes off) |
| **Daypack** | 1 small backpack per person | For water, snacks, purchases, umbrella |
| **Sun protection** | Sunglasses, light hat | October sun can still be strong |
| **Tech** | Portable charger (10,000+ mAh), charging cables, adapter | Phones drain fast with maps + photos |
| **Health** | All medications for full trip, basic first aid, motion sickness pills | Bring prescription copies in English |
| **Documents** | Passports, flight printouts, hotel addresses in Japanese, travel insurance docs | Laminate or use waterproof pouch |
| **Misc** | Reusable water bottle, zip-lock bags (for receipts/coins), small towel/handkerchief | Many restrooms lack hand dryers; Japanese carry handkerchiefs |

**Do NOT overpack.** Japan has excellent laundry facilities (coin laundry everywhere, many Airbnbs have washers), and you can buy anything you forgot at konbini or 100-yen shops.

## 4. Internet: eSIM vs Pocket WiFi

### Comparison Table

| Feature | eSIM | Pocket WiFi |
|---------|------|-------------|
| **Setup** | Download before departure, activate on arrival | Pick up at airport counter or receive by mail |
| **Devices connected** | 1 per eSIM (can hotspot to share) | 5-10 devices simultaneously |
| **Battery** | Uses your phone's battery | Separate device, needs daily charging (lasts ~8-12 hrs) |
| **Carrying** | Nothing extra to carry | Extra device + charger |
| **Cost (16 days)** | $15-40 per device | $55-90 for one device |
| **Data** | 1GB/day to unlimited depending on plan | Usually unlimited |
| **Coverage** | SoftBank or Docomo network | SoftBank, Docomo, or AU network |
| **Return** | Nothing to return | Must return at airport or by mail |
| **Phone number** | Usually no (data only) | No |

### Recommendation for Family of 4

**Option A: 2 eSIMs + hotspot sharing (BEST VALUE)**
- Buy 2 eSIMs (one per adult phone), share data with kids via hotspot
- Total cost: ~$50-80 for 16 days
- Providers: **Airalo**, **Ubigi**, **MobiMatter**, or **Sim Local**
- Works if kids' phones support WiFi

**Option B: 1 Pocket WiFi + 1 eSIM (BEST FOR SPLITTING UP)**
- Pocket WiFi shared by kids + 1 parent; eSIM for other parent
- Useful when splitting up (e.g., dad takes son to arcade, mom takes daughter shopping)
- Total cost: ~$70-100

**Option C: Pocket WiFi only (SIMPLEST)**
- One device, everyone connects
- Must stay together; no internet if device runs out of battery
- Total cost: ~$55-90

> **Tip**: Check if your Israeli phone supports eSIM before departure. Most phones from 2020+ do (iPhone XS and later, Samsung Galaxy S20 and later, Google Pixel 3a and later). Buy and install the eSIM profile at home before your flight -- just don't activate it until you land in Japan.

## 5. Tax-Free Shopping

### Current System (Valid During Your Oct 1-17 Trip)

Japan's current tax-free shopping system is **still in effect** during your visit. The new refund-based system takes effect **November 1, 2026** -- you travel in October, so you benefit from the simpler current system.

### How It Works (Until Oct 31, 2026)

| Rule | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Minimum purchase** | 5,000 yen (before tax) at a single store in a single day |
| **Tax saved** | 10% consumption tax removed at register |
| **Who qualifies** | Foreign tourists on a "temporary visitor" visa (standard tourist entry) |
| **What you need** | Passport (staff will scan it at purchase) |
| **What's eligible** | Most goods: electronics, clothing, cosmetics, food, souvenirs |
| **Consumables** (food, cosmetics, drinks) | Must be sealed in a special bag; do NOT open in Japan |
| **General items** (electronics, clothes) | No seal required; can use during your stay |

### Where to Shop Tax-Free

| Store | Tax-Free? | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| **Don Quijote (Donki)** | Yes | Show passport at register. Download the "Visit Japan Web" app for faster processing. |
| **BIC Camera** | Yes | Electronics + much more. Tax-free counter on each floor. |
| **Yodobashi Camera** | Yes | Similar to BIC Camera |
| **Uniqlo** | Yes | Flagship stores (Ginza, Ikebukuro) have tax-free counters |
| **Matsumoto Kiyoshi** (drugstore) | Yes | Cosmetics, snacks, medicine. Very popular with tourists. |
| **Animate / Mandarake** | Some locations | Ask at register; not all anime shops participate |
| **Pokemon Center** | Yes | Tax-free available at major locations |
| **Department stores** (Takashimaya, Isetan) | Yes | Dedicated tax-free counter, usually on one specific floor |

### Important Notes

- The 5,000 yen minimum is **per store, per day** -- you cannot combine purchases across stores
- Sealed consumable bags: customs may check at departure. If the seal is broken, you owe the tax
- Keep all tax-free receipts with your passport until departure
- **After November 1, 2026**: The system changes to a refund model (pay tax at register, claim refund at airport customs before departure). This does NOT affect your October trip.

## 6. New 2026 Departure Tax

### What Changed

Starting **July 1, 2026**, Japan tripled the international departure tax from 1,000 yen to **3,000 yen per person**.

| Detail | Info |
|--------|------|
| **Amount** | 3,000 yen per person (~$20 / ~74 NIS) |
| **Who pays** | Everyone aged 2+ departing Japan by air or sea |
| **Exempt** | Infants under 2, transit passengers (<24 hours) |
| **How it's collected** | Included in your airline ticket price (you won't pay separately) |
| **Family of 4 total** | 12,000 yen (~$80 / ~296 NIS) |

### Impact on Your Budget

Since you depart October 17, you **will** pay the new rate. For your family of 4, that is 12,000 yen added to your ticket costs. Most airlines have already incorporated this into ticket pricing, so you likely won't see it as a separate charge -- but it is reflected in higher base fares compared to pre-July 2026 prices.

## 7. Cash vs Card & ATMs

### The Reality in 2026

Japan has become significantly more card-friendly since 2020, but cash is still important.

| Payment Method | Where It Works | Where It Doesn't |
|----------------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Credit/Debit Cards** (Visa, Mastercard) | Major stores, department stores, chain restaurants, convenience stores, train stations | Small ramen shops, street food stalls, temple markets, vending machines (some), Nakamise Street shops, some small anime shops |
| **IC Card (Suica/Pasmo)** | All trains, buses, convenience stores, vending machines, many shops | Not accepted everywhere; mainly transport + konbini |
| **Cash (yen)** | Everywhere, always | -- |

### How Much Cash to Carry

| Scenario | Recommended Cash |
|----------|-----------------|
| **Arrival day** | 50,000 yen per family (~$333) for first 2-3 days |
| **Daily carry** | 10,000-15,000 yen per family for street food, small shops, temples |
| **ATM withdrawals** | Top up every 3-4 days as needed |

### Where to Get Cash (7-Eleven ATMs)

**7-Eleven ATMs are your best friend.** They are everywhere, accept international cards, and have English menus.

| ATM Feature | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| **Location** | Every 7-Eleven (there is one on virtually every block) |
| **Hours** | Most 24/7; some close midnight-6 AM |
| **Cards accepted** | Visa, Mastercard, Maestro, Cirrus, Plus (virtually all international cards) |
| **Withdrawal limit** | Usually 50,000-100,000 yen per transaction |
| **Fees** | 110 yen per withdrawal from 7-Eleven side; your bank may charge additional fees |
| **Language** | Full English interface available |

**Also works**: Japan Post ATMs (at post offices), Lawson ATMs, Family Mart ATMs

### Tips for Israeli Travelers

- **Notify your Israeli bank** before departure (Leumi, Hapoalim, Discount, Mizrahi) that you are traveling to Japan
- Check your card's **foreign transaction fee** (usually 2-3%)
- Consider getting a **travel card** or **Revolut/Wise card** for better exchange rates and lower fees
- **Shekel-to-yen** exchange is best done via ATM in Japan, NOT at Ben Gurion airport (terrible rates)
- Bring a small amount of yen from Israel for the airport arrival (transit to city, first meal) -- or withdraw immediately at Narita/Haneda airport 7-Eleven

## 9. Electricity & Adapters

### Israel vs Japan Power Comparison

| Specification | Israel | Japan | Compatible? |
|---------------|--------|-------|-------------|
| **Plug type** | Type C (Europlug) and Type H (Israeli 3-pin) | Type A (2 flat pins, like USA) | NO -- adapter required |
| **Voltage** | 230V | 100V | Most modern chargers (phones, laptops) handle 100-240V automatically |
| **Frequency** | 50Hz | 50Hz (East Japan) / 60Hz (West Japan) | Yes |

### What You Need

**Buy a Type C/H to Type A adapter** -- at least 2-3 for the family (one per charging station).

| Option | Where to Buy | Price |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Universal travel adapter** | Amazon, duty-free at Ben Gurion, or any electronics store in Israel | 30-80 NIS |
| **Simple plug adapter (C to A)** | Amazon, Kravitz, Bug, or airport shops | 15-30 NIS |
| **In Japan** (backup) | Daiso (100 yen), BIC Camera, Don Quijote | 100-500 yen |

### Important Notes

- **Phone chargers, laptop chargers, camera chargers**: All modern USB chargers are 100-240V -- they work with just a plug adapter, no voltage converter needed
- **Hair dryers, curling irons**: Check the label. If it says "100-240V" you are fine with just an adapter. If it says "220-240V" only, do NOT use it in Japan (100V) -- it will work very slowly or may overheat. Better to leave it home and use the hotel's.
- **Bring a small power strip** from Israel + one adapter = charge all devices from one outlet

## 10. Tipping: DO NOT TIP

This is one of the most important cultural rules in Japan.

### The Rule

**Do not tip anyone, ever, under any circumstances.**

| Situation | Tip? | What to Do Instead |
|-----------|------|-------------------|
| Restaurants | NO | Simply pay the bill amount. Say "gochisousama deshita" (thank you for the meal) |
| Taxis | NO | Pay the meter amount exactly |
| Hotels | NO | No tip for bellhops, housekeeping, concierge |
| Tour guides | NO | A small gift (from Israel!) is appreciated but never cash |
| Hair salons / services | NO | Just pay the listed price |
| Bars | NO | Pay for your drinks only |

### Why?

In Japanese culture, excellent service is the baseline expectation, not something that requires extra payment. Tipping can be seen as:
- **Insulting** -- implying the person needs charity
- **Confusing** -- staff may chase you down to return the "forgotten" money
- **Rude** -- suggesting their salary is insufficient

### The Exception

If you absolutely must show gratitude (e.g., a ryokan host who went above and beyond), place cash in a **clean, sealed envelope** and present it with both hands and a bow. This is called "kokorozuke" and is extremely rare even among Japanese people.

## 13. Luggage Forwarding (Takkyubin)

### What is Takkyubin?

Takkyubin (or takuhaibin) is Japan's legendary luggage delivery service. You can send suitcases between hotels, airports, and cities for a fraction of what you'd pay for a taxi -- and travel hands-free.

### How to Use It

| Step | Details |
|------|---------|
| **1. Where to drop off** | Hotel front desk, convenience store (7-Eleven, Family Mart), airport counter (Yamato Transport / Sagawa) |
| **2. Fill out the form** | Staff will help. You need: sender name, destination name/address, phone number |
| **3. Pay** | Cash at convenience store; cash or card at airport counters |
| **4. Delivery time** | Same-day within Tokyo (if sent by morning), next-day for intercity |
| **5. Pick up** | It will be waiting at your hotel front desk |

### Costs

| Route | Size | Price (approx.) |
|-------|------|-----------------|
| **Within Tokyo** (hotel to hotel) | Small (up to 60cm) | 930-1,100 yen |
| **Within Tokyo** | Large (up to 160cm total) | 1,500-2,440 yen |
| **Tokyo to Osaka** | Small | 1,100-1,400 yen |
| **Tokyo to Osaka** | Large | 1,700-2,800 yen |
| **Hotel to Airport** (Narita/Haneda) | Standard suitcase | 1,500-3,000 yen |

## 14. Coin Lockers

### Overview

Coin lockers are available at virtually every train station, shopping area, and tourist spot in Tokyo. Perfect for storing bags during day trips.

### Sizes & Prices

| Size | Dimensions (H x W x D) | Fits | Price (per day) |
|------|------------------------|------|-----------------|
| **Small (S)** | 35 x 34 x 57 cm | Small backpack, shopping bags | 300-400 yen |
| **Medium (M)** | 57 x 34 x 57 cm | Carry-on suitcase, large backpack | 400-500 yen |
| **Large (L)** | 87 x 34 x 57 cm | Medium suitcase | 500-700 yen |
| **Extra Large (LL)** | 117 x 34 x 57 cm | Large suitcase | 700-1,000 yen |

### How They Work

1. **Find an empty locker** (green/unlocked indicator light)
2. **Put your bags in**, close the door
3. **Pay** -- most accept IC cards (Suica/Pasmo) or 100-yen coins
4. **Take your key** (physical key) or **remember your locker number** (IC card locks to your card)
5. **To retrieve**: insert key or tap IC card, door opens
6. **Billing**: Per calendar day (midnight to midnight), not per 24 hours. Bags left past midnight incur a second day charge.

### Key Locker Locations

| Station | Number of Lockers | Notes |
|---------|-------------------|-------|
| **Tokyo Station** | 1,500+ lockers | Most lockers of any station in Japan. Multiple areas -- use the "Coin Locker Navi" app to find empty ones. |
| **Shinjuku Station** | 3,600+ lockers | Most in terms of quantity. Spread across a massive station -- look near exits. |
| **Ikebukuro Station** | 500+ lockers | Near your home base. East and West exit areas. |
| **Shibuya Station** | 400+ lockers | Recently renovated station has new locker areas. |
| **Ueno Station** | 300+ lockers | Near Ueno Park entrance. |
| **Asakusa Station** | 200+ lockers | Near Senso-ji. Fills up fast on weekends. |

### Tips

- **Large lockers fill up fast** at popular stations (especially by 10 AM on weekends). Arrive early or have a backup plan.
- **IC card payment is easier** -- no need to carry 100-yen coins, and you can't lose a physical key
- **3-day maximum** -- most lockers have a 3-day storage limit. After that, contents are removed and held at the station office.
- **Alternative**: If lockers are full, look for **ecbo cloak** (luggage storage service app) -- partner shops and cafes store your bags for 400-700 yen/day. Works via app.
- **Oversized bags**: If your suitcase doesn't fit any locker, use Takkyubin (see section above) or ecbo cloak.
