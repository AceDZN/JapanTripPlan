### 1.1 PokePark Kanto (NEW -- Opened February 5, 2026)

The **world's first permanent Pokemon theme park**, located inside Yomiuriland amusement park. This is the single most important new attraction for your son.

**Location**: Yomiuriland, straddling Tokyo and Kanagawa Prefecture
**Access**: Less than 30 minutes from Shinjuku Station (Keio Line to Keio-Yomiuriland Station, then gondola or bus)
**Size**: 26,000 square meters
**Website**: https://www.pokepark-kanto.co.jp/ppark/top/index?languageKind=en_US

#### Park Layout

The park is divided into two main areas:

| Area | Description | Highlights |
|------|-------------|------------|
| **Sedge Town** | A Pokemon town with shops, shows, and attractions | Sedge Gym (live shows), Pokemon Center, Daisuki Shop, Pokemon Playhouse, Sedge Condominiums |
| **Pokemon Forest** | An immersive forest area with 600+ Pokemon to discover | Find and interact with Pokemon characters in a themed natural environment |

#### Ticket Types & Prices

| Pass Type | Adult (13+) | Child (3-12) | What's Included |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Town Pass** | From 4,700 | From 2,800 | Sedge Town entry, Gym show lottery, Playhouse photo ops |
| **Trainer's Pass** | From 7,900 | From 4,700 | Town Pass + single timed entry to Pokemon Forest |
| **Ace Trainer's Pass** | From 14,000 | From 11,000 | Town + unlimited Forest + priority lanes + reserved Gym show seating + Condominiums |

*Prices in JPY. All passes include entry to Yomiuriland. Prices vary by day (weekdays cheaper, weekends/holidays higher).*

**Recommended for your family**: Trainer's Pass is the best value. The Ace Trainer's Pass is worth it only if your son is absolutely obsessed and you want the full VIP experience (priority lanes, unlimited forest access).

### 2.3 Nakano Broadway -- The Budget Anime Shopper's Paradise

**Why Nakano Broadway over Akihabara**: Prices are **20-40% cheaper** for the same goods. This is where Tokyo locals shop for anime merchandise. Less touristy, more authentic.

**Location**: 5-minute walk from Nakano Station (North Exit), which is a 5-minute train ride from Shinjuku on the JR Chuo Line
**Hours**: Generally 10:00-20:00 (some shops open at 12:00-13:00, best to visit afternoon)

#### What to Find

| Category | Price Range | Notes |
|----------|------------|-------|
| Used manga | 100-500 per volume | Some deals at 3 volumes for 200 |
| Pre-owned figures | 500-10,000 | Fraction of new prices, excellent condition |
| Trading cards (Pokemon, etc.) | 50-50,000+ | Huge selection of singles and packs |
| Vintage goods | Varies | Rare items from 80s/90s anime |
| Gachapon capsule toys | 200-500 each | Hundreds of machines |

#### Mandarake at Nakano Broadway

Mandarake operates **over 30 specialized stores** spread across floors 2-4 of Nakano Broadway. Each store focuses on a different niche:

- Boys' manga and figures
- Girls' manga and BL (daughter may enjoy this section)
- Vintage toys and collectibles
- Trading cards
- Doujinshi
- Retro video games
- Cels and original art

**Staff speak English** and can help you search for specific items.

**Tax-free shopping** available with passport for purchases over 5,000.

**Budget tip**: This is the best place to buy Pokemon trading cards, Hunter x Hunter figures, and Demon Slayer merch at the lowest prices in Tokyo.

### 2.4 MoN Takanawa -- Museum of Narratives (NEW -- Opens March 28, 2026)

**Location**: Takanawa Gateway City, Minato-ku (next to Takanawa Gateway Station on the JR Yamanote Line)
**Opens**: March 28, 2026
**Opening theme**: "Life as Culture" (runs March-September 2026)
**Architecture**: 6 floors above ground, 3 basement levels. Designed by Kengo Kuma. Stunning timber spiral exterior with native plants.

#### Why Visit

- **MANGALOGUE presentations**: Manga brought to life on giant screens with immersive sound and lighting -- unlike anything else in the world
- Features spanning traditional performing arts, **manga and anime**, music, food
- One of the confirmed MANGALOGUE presentations features Osamu Tezuka's "Phoenix" (Hi no Tori)
- The building itself is architecturally stunning

**Ticket prices**: Not yet announced as of February 2026. Check https://www.montakanawa.jp/en closer to your trip.

**Time needed**: 2-3 hours
**Best combined with**: Nearby areas accessible from Yamanote Line

The magical museum designed by Hayao Miyazaki himself. Small, intimate, and absolutely enchanting.

**Location**: Inokashira Park, Mitaka City (25 min from Shinjuku on JR Chuo Line)
**Duration**: 2-3 hours
**Entry times**: 10:00, 12:00, 14:00, or 16:00
**Website**: https://www.ghibli-museum.jp/en/tickets/

#### Ticket Prices

| Category | Price (JPY) |
|----------|-------------|
| Adult (19+) | 1,000 |
| Youth (13-18) | 700 |
| Child (7-12) | 400 |
| Young child (4-6) | 100 |
| Under 3 | Free |

**Family of 4 cost**: 1,000 + 1,000 + 700 + 400 = **3,100** (~$21) -- incredibly affordable!

#### Highlights Inside

- **Saturn Theater** -- the short film screening room
- **Museum shop** -- exclusive Ghibli merch not available anywhere else
- **Straw Hat Cafe** -- small cafe with themed food

#### Is Ghibli Park Worth the Day Trip?

**Pros**:
- Much larger than the Museum -- a full day experience
- Walk through actual life-size recreations of Howl's Castle, Satsuki & Mei's house
- Less famous among tourists than the Museum -- slightly easier to get tickets
- Beautiful park setting (especially in October with autumn colors)

**Cons**:
- Located near Nagoya -- requires shinkansen (12,000-14,000 per person each way from Tokyo)
- A full day commitment including travel
- You are already visiting Osaka/Kyoto (Oct 12-14) -- Nagoya is between Tokyo and Osaka

**Recommendation**: If you can get tickets, **visit Ghibli Park on your way from Tokyo to Osaka** (Oct 11). Take the shinkansen to Nagoya, spend the day at Ghibli Park, then continue to Osaka by shinkansen in the evening. This eliminates a separate day trip. However, this conflicts with the current night-bus plan. Consider whether the experience is worth the extra shinkansen cost.

**Alternative**: Do Ghibli Museum in Tokyo (easier logistics) and skip Ghibli Park, unless you are hardcore Ghibli fans.

### Money-Saving Tips

1. **Nakano Broadway prices beat Akihabara by 20-40%** -- buy the bulk of figures and cards here
2. **Tax-free shopping**: Spend 5,000+ at a single store, show passport, save 10% consumption tax
3. **Mandarake pre-owned = gold**: Figures in A/B condition at 30-60% off retail. Inspect carefully -- quality is usually excellent
4. **Time your Pokemon Center visit**: Go on weekday mornings for best selection of limited items
5. **Don Quijote for budget gifts**: Anime snacks, small stationery, keychains -- cheap and everyone back home will love them
6. **100-yen shops (Daiso, Can-Do, Seria)**: Surprisingly good anime-adjacent goods (stationery, snack containers, small figures)
7. **Gachapon strategy**: Budget 2,000-3,000 total across the whole trip. It is easy to blow 5,000 in one session
8. **Compare prices with your phone**: Take photos of prices at the first store. Check Mandarake, Surugaya, and Book Off before buying expensive items
9. **Pokemon 30th anniversary items**: Expect special items at EVERY Pokemon Center. Prioritize Japan-exclusive items you cannot buy online
10. **Luggage space**: Leave room in your suitcase! Or buy a cheap expandable bag at Don Quijote for souvenirs
